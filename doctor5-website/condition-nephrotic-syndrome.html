Nephrotic Syndrome & Severe Proteinuria

Recognizing the tetrad of heavy protein leak, low blood albumin, edema, and hyperlipidemia.

What is Nephrotic Syndrome?

Nephrotic syndrome is not a single disease, but a clinical syndrome resulting from severe damage to the podocyte cells and basement membrane of the kidney's filtering units. When podocyte slit diaphragms break down, massive quantities of serum albumin leak out into urine.

Loss of albumin reduces oncotic pressure in blood vessels, causing fluid to seep out into bodily tissues—producing dramatic swelling around the eyes, face, ankles, abdomen (ascites), and pleural spaces around the lungs.

The Classic Clinical Tetrad:

1. Heavy Proteinuria: Exceeding 3.5 grams per 24 hours.
2. Hypoalbuminemia: Serum albumin dropping below 3.0 g/dL.
3. Generalized Edema: Facial puffiness in the morning; bilateral pitting leg swelling by evening.
4. Hypercholesterolemia: The liver compensates for low albumin by producing excess lipids and clotting factors.

Underlying Causes in Adults & Children

Identifying the precise histopathological diagnosis is paramount, as treatments vary significantly:

  • Minimal Change Disease (MCD): Most common cause in children; highly steroid-responsive.
  • Membranous Nephropathy (MN): Leading cause in older adults; often linked to anti-PLA2R antibodies.
  • Focal Segmental Glomerulosclerosis (FSGS): Scarring of segments of glomeruli; requires targeted immunosuppression.
  • Lupus Nephritis: Kidney involvement in systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE).
  • Diabetic Glomerulosclerosis: Advanced diabetic kidney architecture changes.

Role of Kidney Biopsy

In adult nephrotic syndrome, an ultrasound-guided percutaneous renal biopsy is usually essential. A tiny sample of kidney tissue is examined under light microscopy, immunofluorescence, and electron microscopy. This pinpoints the exact autoimmune mechanism and guides precise therapy with corticosteroids, calcineurin inhibitors, or monoclonal antibodies (e.g., Rituximab).
