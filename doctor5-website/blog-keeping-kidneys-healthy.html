Keeping Your Kidneys Healthy: Everyday Habits That Matter

By Dr. Aishwarya Lal, Consultant Nephrologist • October 2026 • 4 min read

Small Daily Choices, Decades of Kidney Vitality

Every single day, your pair of kidneys filters approximately 180 liters of fluid, selectively reabsorbing vital minerals and discarding cellular waste products. While medical science offers advanced treatments once disease strikes, prevention remains infinitely more powerful and accessible.

Here are the fundamental, evidence-backed lifestyle habits Dr. Aishwarya Lal emphasizes to keep your kidneys robust throughout your lifespan.

1. Drink Sensibly, Not Excessively

There is a widespread myth that drinking 5 or 6 liters of water daily is beneficial for everyone. In truth, an average adult in moderate climates needs approximately 2 to 2.5 liters of clean fluid daily. Staying well-hydrated helps kidneys clear sodium and urea without straining. (Note: Patients with advanced CKD, heart failure, or on dialysis must follow fluid restriction prescribed by their doctor).

2. Tame the Hidden Salt in Your Food

High sodium intake directly forces kidneys to work harder, retains fluid, and drives blood pressure upward. Aim for under 5 grams of salt per day (approximately one level teaspoon). Be vigilant about hidden sodium lurking in restaurant gravies, bakery goods, pickles, papads, and processed snacks.

3. Beware of Over-the-Counter Painkillers (NSAIDs)

One of the most common causes of preventable Acute Kidney Injury in outpatient practice is the casual use of non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) like ibuprofen, naproxen, and diclofenac. These drugs constrict the afferent renal blood vessels, abruptly decreasing kidney filtration—especially when you are dehydrated. Use paracetamol for minor aches unless your doctor explicitly advises otherwise.

4. Keep Your Blood Pressure Below 130/80 mmHg

High blood pressure is the second leading cause of kidney failure. Have your blood pressure checked regularly. If you are prescribed antihypertensive medication, take it faithfully every day—even if you feel completely healthy.

5. Don't Let Blood Sugar Roam Free

For individuals with diabetes, maintaining an HbA1c around 6.5%–7.0% protects the delicate microvascular basement membranes in your filtering nephrons. Pair glucose control with annual urine microalbumin testing.

6. Avoid Unverified "Herbal" Concoctions

Certain traditional, unregulated supplements contain heavy metals (mercury, lead, arsenic) or aristolochic acid, which cause irreversible interstitial nephritis and renal scarring. Always consult a qualified medical specialist before taking supplements.
