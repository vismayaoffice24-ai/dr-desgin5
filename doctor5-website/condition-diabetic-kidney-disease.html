Diabetic Kidney Disease (Diabetic Nephropathy)

Understanding the link between diabetes and kidney health, early warning signs, and modern protection strategies.

Why Does Diabetes Affect the Kidneys?

Each human kidney contains roughly one million microscopic filtering units called nephrons, centered around delicate clusters of capillaries known as glomeruli. Over years of elevated blood sugar, toxic advanced glycation end-products (AGEs) deposit in glomerular basement membranes, causing thickening, stiffness, and chronic low-grade inflammation.

Concurrently, elevated sugar causes the afferent arterioles feeding each filter to dilate, subjecting capillaries to hyperfiltration and high pressure. Over time, filtering membranes leak albumin and eventually undergo permanent glomerulosclerosis (scarring).

Early Warning Signs You Must Not Ignore:

• Foamy or Frothy Urine: Excess lather that requires multiple flushes, indicating albumin leak.
• Rising Blood Pressure: Sudden increase in blood pressure readings that previously were well-controlled.
• Dependent Edema: Mild swelling around ankles or socks leaving deep indentations on your skin.
• Decreasing Need for Insulin/Oral Medications: When kidneys fail, they excrete insulin slower, paradoxically causing unexpected low blood sugars.

How Do We Screen for Diabetic Kidney Disease?

Relying only on serum creatinine is dangerous, because creatinine usually remains normal until up to 50% of kidney function is already damaged. Dr. Aishwarya Lal recommends:

  • Spot Urine Albumin-to-Creatinine Ratio (uACR): The gold standard screening test. Detects microalbuminuria (30–300 mg/g) years before creatinine increases.
  • Serum Creatinine & eGFR Calculation: Performed alongside uACR to assess overall filtration rate.
  • Dilated Eye Examination: Because diabetic nephropathy and diabetic retinopathy share identical microvascular pathology, signs of retinal damage almost always signal concurrent kidney vulnerability.
