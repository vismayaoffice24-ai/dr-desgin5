Diabetes and Kidney Protection

Diabetes is the leading cause of chronic kidney disease and kidney failure across the world, accounting for nearly 40% of all dialysis cases. Chronic high blood sugar damages the fragile microscopic capillaries (glomeruli) inside the kidneys over years, initially causing microscopic protein leakage before progressively elevating serum creatinine.

At Dr. Aishwarya Lal's Diabetic Kidney Care Clinic, our emphasis is on catching damage at the microalbuminuria stage—when it is still reversible or completely stabilizable.

The Natural Progression of Diabetic Nephropathy:

• Hyperfiltration (Stage 1): Kidneys work abnormally hard; blood tests seem normal.
• Microalbuminuria (Stage 2): 30–300 mg/g albumin in urine. Critical early alert window.
• Macroalbuminuria (Stage 3): >300 mg/g protein in urine; hypertension emerges.
• Overt Nephropathy (Stage 4 & 5): Rising creatinine, declining eGFR, edema.

Modern Kidney-Protective Therapies

Kidney medicine has undergone a revolutionary shift in diabetic care. Today, we utilize targeted therapies that protect the kidneys independent of blood glucose lowering alone:

  • SGLT2 Inhibitors (Dapagliflozin, Empagliflozin): These breakthrough agents lower intraglomerular pressure, reduce proteinuria by up to 40%, and significantly delay the need for dialysis.
  • Non-Steroidal Mineralocorticoid Receptor Antagonists (Finerenone): Blocks inflammation and fibrosis in the kidney parenchyma.
  • Renin-Angiotensin System Blockade (ACEi / ARBs): Lowers glomerular pressure and stabilizes microvascular filtering walls.
  • Blood Pressure Control (<130/80 mmHg): Essential to prevent capillary shear stress.
Annual Screening Recommendation:

Every person diagnosed with Type 2 diabetes should undergo a spot urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio (uACR) test and serum creatinine test at least once every 12 months. Early action saves kidneys.
