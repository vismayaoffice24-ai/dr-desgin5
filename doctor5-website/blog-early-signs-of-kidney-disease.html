Early Signs of Kidney Disease You Shouldn't Ignore

By Dr. Aishwarya Lal, Consultant Nephrologist • October 2026 • 5 min read

The Silent Progression of Kidney Disease

One of the most insidious aspects of kidney disease is its quiet beginning. Unlike an injured joint or an upset stomach, failing kidneys rarely ache or send sharp pain alerts. In fact, most people can lose up to two-thirds of their overall kidney function before experiencing severe distress.

Because early detection allows us to halt or dramatically slow progression, learning to recognize subtle bodily signals is critical for every adult.

Key Takeaway:

If you have diabetes, high blood pressure, or a family history of kidney trouble, do not wait for symptoms. Annual blood and urine tests are essential.

1. Puffy Eyes Upon Waking (Periorbital Edema)

Do you frequently wake up with noticeable swelling or puffiness around your eyelids that persists through the morning? When kidney filtering units become leaky, protein (albumin) spills into the urine rather than remaining in the bloodstream. Low blood albumin causes fluid to pool in loose facial tissues overnight.

2. Foamy, Frothy, or Bubbly Urine

Urine that looks frothy—resembling whipped egg whites or beer foam—and requires two or three flushes to clear is a classic sign of proteinuria. While occasional rapid urination can create temporary bubbles, persistent thick foam is abnormal.

3. Persistent Swelling in Ankles and Feet

Kidneys are responsible for balancing sodium and water. When filtration slows, the body retains excess salt and fluid, which gravitational pull settles into your lower extremities. You may notice your socks leaving deep grooves in your shins or your shoes feeling unusually tight by late afternoon.

4. Unexplained Chronic Fatigue and Brain Fog

Healthy kidneys produce an essential hormone called erythropoietin (EPO), which instructs your bone marrow to create oxygen-carrying red blood cells. As kidney function diminishes, EPO production drops, leading to renal anemia. Patients frequently report overwhelming tiredness, shortness of breath on stairs, and difficulty concentrating.

5. Nocturia (Waking Frequently to Urinate)

Waking up two or more times every night to pass urine can indicate that your kidneys have lost their ability to concentrate urine during sleep. While prostate enlargement or urinary infections can also cause nocturia, kidney dysfunction must be ruled out.

6. Dry, Intensely Itchy Skin

As kidneys struggle to filter wastes, mineral and bone metabolism becomes disturbed. The buildup of phosphorus and uremic toxins in the skin can trigger deep, persistent itching that moisturizing lotions cannot relieve.

What Should You Do Next?

If you recognize two or more of these warning signs, ask your doctor for two simple, inexpensive tests: a Serum Creatinine test (with eGFR) and a Urine Albumin-to-Creatinine Ratio (uACR). Early intervention can preserve your kidneys for a lifetime.
