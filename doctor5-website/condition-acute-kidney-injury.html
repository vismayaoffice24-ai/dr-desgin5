Acute Kidney Injury (AKI)

A sudden drop in renal function over hours or days: Recognizing causes, emergency treatment, and recovery.

What is Acute Kidney Injury?

Unlike Chronic Kidney Disease which develops slowly over years, Acute Kidney Injury (AKI) is an abrupt decline in kidney filtration occurring within 48 hours to 7 days. It manifests as a rapid spike in serum creatinine, an abrupt drop in urine output, and accumulation of nitrogenous waste products.

The critical difference: While advanced CKD is irreversible, AKI is frequently fully reversible if the underlying cause is diagnosed and treated promptly before tubular cells suffer permanent necrosis.

Three Categories of AKI Causes:

1. Prerenal AKI (Decreased Blood Flow): Severe dehydration, profuse vomiting/diarrhea, heat stroke, excessive diuretic use, heart failure, or major blood loss.
2. Intrinsic Renal AKI (Direct Tissue Damage): Sepsis, severe infections, nephrotoxic medications (NSAID painkillers, aminoglycosides, IV contrast dye), or acute interstitial nephritis.
3. Postrenal AKI (Urinary Obstruction): Bilateral kidney stones, enlarged prostate (BPH), or pelvic tumors blocking urine outflow.

Key Symptoms of Acute Kidney Injury

  • Oliguria: Substantially diminished urine output (less than 400–500 mL over 24 hours), or in severe cases, complete absence of urine (anuria).
  • Rapid Fluid Overload: Swelling in the lower legs and ankles, or acute shortness of breath from pulmonary edema.
  • Neurological Changes: Mental confusion, lethargy, drowsiness, or tremors due to uremic toxin buildup.
  • Nausea & Vomiting: Inability to tolerate meals or oral medications.
  • Arrhythmias: Dangerous irregular heartbeats caused by severe hyperkalemia (high blood potassium).
Immediate Medical Emergency:

Suspected AKI requires immediate clinical evaluation and hospitalization. Early fluid optimization, withdrawal of offending medications, and temporary hemodialysis support can save lives and restore normal kidney function.
