What is Dialysis? A Gentle Guide for Families

By Dr. Aishwarya Lal, Consultant Nephrologist • October 2026 • 7 min read

Removing the Fear Around Dialysis

For many families, hearing the word "dialysis" triggers feelings of helplessness and distress. Pop culture often portrays dialysis as a bleak, painful confinement. In reality, modern renal replacement therapy is a medical marvel that restores vitality, removes life-threatening toxins and fluid, and allows millions of individuals worldwide to continue working, laughing, and enjoying their families.

Dialysis does not mean the end of life; it is a life-sustaining treatment that takes over the filtering work that diseased kidneys can no longer perform on their own.

When Does Someone Need Dialysis?

Dialysis is generally considered when a patient reaches Stage 5 Chronic Kidney Disease—typically when kidney function drops below 10% to 15% (eGFR <15 mL/min) and symptoms of uremia appear:

  • Severe fluid retention in the lungs (pulmonary edema) causing breathlessness.
  • Dangerously elevated blood potassium (hyperkalemia) that medications cannot stabilize.
  • Intractable nausea, metallic taste, severe loss of appetite, and significant involuntary weight loss.
  • Uremic pericarditis (inflammation around the heart) or severe encephalopathy (drowsiness and confusion).
The Two Modalities Explained:

• Hemodialysis (In-Center): Blood is withdrawn through a special vascular connection (AV fistula), passed through a sterile artificial filter (dialyzer) bathed in dialysate fluid, and returned clean to the body. This is typically done 2 to 3 times per week for about 4 hours per session.

• Peritoneal Dialysis (At-Home): A soft, flexible catheter is placed into the abdominal cavity. Special sterile cleansing fluid is introduced, remains in the abdomen for a few hours where your peritoneal membrane filters waste, and is then drained. This can be done manually during the day (CAPD) or automatically overnight by a cycler machine (APD).

Why AV Fistula Planning is the Best Decision

If you or your family member chooses hemodialysis, having an Arteriovenous (AV) Fistula created 3 to 6 months in advance is the safest decision. An AV fistula is a minor surgical joining of an artery and a vein in your forearm. It uses your own natural vessels, has the lowest risk of infection, and lasts for years—completely avoiding temporary plastic neck catheters (central venous lines).
