Kidney Stone Management & Prevention

In-depth metabolic evaluation, dietary stone risk analysis, and prevention of recurrent stone attacks.

Why Nephrology Care Matters for Kidney Stones

While urologists excel at surgical removal of obstructive stones (via laser lithotripsy or PCNL), a nephrologist focuses on why your body creates stones in the first place and how to permanently prevent them. Over 50% of patients who develop a kidney stone suffer another episode within 5 years unless metabolic root causes are identified and corrected.

At Dr. Aishwarya Lal's clinic, we conduct thorough biochemical and metabolic investigations to protect your renal parenchyma and keep you stone-free.

Common Types of Kidney Stones:

• Calcium Oxalate (75%–80%): Formed when calcium binds with oxalate in concentrated urine.
• Uric Acid (5%–10%): Associated with acidic urine, high purine intake, or metabolic syndrome.
• Struvite / Infection Stones: Caused by bacterial urinary tract infections producing urease.
• Cystine Stones (<1%): Rare hereditary metabolic disorder of cystine transport.

Our Metabolic Diagnostic Protocol

  • 24-Hour Urine Metabolic Profile: Measuring total volume, calcium, oxalate, citrate, uric acid, sodium, and pH to pinpoint exact chemical imbalances.
  • Blood Chemistry: Checking serum calcium, intact Parathyroid Hormone (PTH), uric acid, and creatinine to rule out hyperparathyroidism or renal dysfunction.
  • Stone Composition Analysis: Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy of retrieved stone fragments.
  • Kidney Ultrasound & Low-Dose CT Review: Evaluating stone size, location, and potential urinary tract hydronephrosis.

Targeted Prevention & Medical Dissolution

  1. Calibrated Hydration Strategy: Designing a fluid regimen to ensure at least 2.5 liters of diluted urine output daily.
  2. Citrate Supplementation: Utilizing potassium citrate to raise urine pH and inhibit crystal agglomeration.
  3. Uric Acid Dissolution: Medical alkalinization protocols that can safely dissolve select radiolucent uric acid stones without surgery.
  4. Dietary Recalibration: Balancing dietary calcium with meals, moderating dietary oxalate and animal purines, and reducing sodium intake to diminish calcium excretion.
