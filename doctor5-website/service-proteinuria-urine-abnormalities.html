Urine & Protein Leak Evaluation

Expert investigation of foamy urine, microscopic and visible hematuria, and nephrotic syndrome.

What Foamy or Discolored Urine Means

Healthy kidney glomeruli function as microscopic sieves with a negative electrical charge that prevents crucial blood proteins like albumin from spilling into urine. When these filters are inflamed, scarred, or mechanically damaged, albumin leaks through into the urinary tract, creating noticeable frothiness or foam that requires multiple flushes to clear.

Similarly, the presence of red blood cells (hematuria)—whether invisible to the naked eye (microscopic) or causing pink, tea-colored, or cola-colored urine (gross hematuria)—requires prompt nephrological differentiation between kidney parenchymal diseases and lower urinary tract urological sources.

Common Conditions Evaluated at This Clinic:

• Nephrotic Syndrome: Massive proteinuria (>3.5g/day), low serum albumin, elevated cholesterol, and severe bodily swelling.
• Glomerulonephritis: Inflammation of filtering units (IgA Nephropathy, Lupus Nephritis, Membranous Nephropathy, FSGS).
• Asymptomatic Hematuria: Red blood cells detected on routine urinalysis without pain.
• Orthostatic & Transient Proteinuria: Harmless postural leaks differentiated from progressive kidney disorders.

Our Diagnostic Approach

  • 24-Hour Urine Protein or Spot Urine Protein/Creatinine Ratio (uPCR): Accurate quantification of total protein loss per day.
  • Phase-Contrast Urine Microscopy: Differentiating dysmorphic glomerular red blood cells and red cell casts from non-glomerular urological bleeding.
  • Autoimmune & Serological Workup: ANA, anti-dsDNA, complement levels (C3, C4), ANCA, anti-PLA2R, and viral hepatitis/HIV serologies.
  • Kidney Biopsy Guidance: When indicated, Dr. Aishwarya coordinates ultrasound-guided percutaneous renal biopsies to obtain an exact tissue diagnosis for targeted immunotherapy.
